EU Fuel Price Response to the Strait of Hormuz Closure

Weekly retail fuel prices in the Netherlands, Germany and Belgium. Event date: 28 February 2026. Window: six months before the event through 2026-09-07.

Economic interpretation

Gasoline: Belgium recorded the largest latest increase (+28.9%), while Netherlands recorded the smallest (+19.6%). Post-event volatility was highest in Germany (weekly price SD 0.1218 €/L).

Diesel: Belgium recorded the largest latest increase (+38.5%), while Netherlands recorded the smallest (+30.4%). Post-event volatility was highest in Germany (weekly price SD 0.1940 €/L).

The indexed and percentage-change views are economically more informative for cross-country comparison because they remove differences in starting price levels and highlight heterogeneous pass-through after the common shock.

How to read this as an event study

A common external oil-market shock should affect all three countries, but the size and timing of retail pass-through can differ because of taxation, refinery structure, inventories, contracts, exchange-rate exposure and domestic competition.

This dashboard is descriptive, not causal. A causal paper would add Brent crude prices, taxes/excise duties, exchange rates and country fixed effects, then estimate dynamic pre/post coefficients.

Methodology

Baseline = each country's last weekly observation before 28 February 2026. Index = 100 × price / baseline price. Percentage change = 100 × (price / baseline price − 1). Volatility = standard deviation of weekly retail prices in the corresponding period.

Source: uploaded EU Energy Prices weekly fuel-price dataset. This presentation compares retail road-fuel prices, not crude-oil prices.
